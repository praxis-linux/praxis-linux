# The Praxis Manifesto

The desktop has not changed in thirty years.

We open one application to look at files. Another to write words. Another to write code. Another to send messages. We learn to translate our intent into a thousand small mechanical motions: clicks, drags, key combinations, menu paths. Over time, we stop noticing. We become operators of our own machines.

Then large language models arrived, and the industry's response was to bolt them onto the side. A chat sidebar. A "Copilot" button. An assistant that answers questions *about* the work, but cannot become the work.

We believe this is the wrong shape for what comes next.

---

**Praxis is a Linux distribution where the desktop is the agent.**

*Praxis* — from the Greek πρᾶξις — means action: the moment thought becomes deed. Aristotle named it the highest form of activity. We chose the name deliberately. So much of software is theory — settings to be planned, configurations to be debated, dashboards to be admired. Praxis is what happens when intent meets the machine and the machine *does the thing*.

There is no separate AI. There is no chat window beside the real work. There is one surface — the **Canvas** — that listens, understands, and *becomes* whatever the moment requires.

Talking about a folder? It is your file explorer.
Editing a function? It is your IDE.
Drafting a proposal? It is your document.
Tuning the system? It is your settings panel.

The interface is generated from intent, not selected from a menu.

---

## What we believe

**1. The desktop should listen first.** The default input is intent — typed or spoken — not a sequence of clicks. Apps are answers, not destinations.

**2. Local before cloud.** Small models run on your hardware for routine work. Large models in the cloud are called when needed, with your permission, and never without an audible click of the audit log.

**3. Open all the way down.** Open weights where possible. Open protocols (MCP, A2UI). Open license (Apache 2.0). No vendor lock. Bring your own model.

**4. The user is sovereign.** Every action the agent takes is legible, reversible, and consented to. Snapshots before changes. Audit logs by default. The kill switch is always one keystroke away.

**5. Consistency over novelty.** The Canvas may shape-shift, but it does so from a vetted catalog of components. We choose declarative generative UI over arbitrary HTML. Beauty without surprise. Power without chaos.

---

## What we are not

We are not building a chatbot for Linux.
We are not building "Linux with AI features."
We are not selling your prompts to anyone.
We are not picking a side in the model wars.

We are building the operating system that someone learning to use a computer in 2030 will assume always existed.

If you have spent years bending your hands to the shape of your software — this is for you.
